## Two time coordinates and a dynamical clock momentum

The exterior field u(t) is a real spherical-harmonic scalar on unholed unit S²,
with ω_l=l+1/2. Two meridional mouths have positions q_i, canonical momenta p_i,
proper clocks s_i and their canonical momenta k_i. The neck is a resolved
interval field z(σ,x), 0≤x≤L, with its own time σ and unit propagation speed.
The port samples the endpoint at χ_i(s_i)=ε_i s_i+b_i. Define

    V_i = g/2 [z(χ_i(s_i), x_i) − η_i f(q_i)·u]²,
    μ_i = m + k_i + V_i,
    H_ext = Σ_i sqrt(p_i²+μ_i²) + U_C(q_A−q_B)
            + (P·P + u·K_s u)/2,
    S = ∫dt [P·u_dot + Σ_i(p_i q_i_dot+k_i s_i_dot) − H_ext]
        + ∫dσ [z_dot·M_n z_dot − z·K_n z]/2.

U_C is the smoothed opposite-flux Coulomb potential used in #345's pair model;
its self energy is retained. The new radiative channel is a deformation scalar,
not #345's full Maxwell transverse field. This is a meridional, fixed-neck
constitutive reduction. The dynamical k_i are additional internal clock energies,
not derived GR mouth masses. They are initialized at zero and must not be frozen
or replaced with a post-hoc balancing integral.

Hamilton's equations give q_dot=p/E, s_dot=μ/E=√(1−q_dot²),
k_dot=−(μ/E)∂V/∂s, and p_dot=−∂U_C/∂q−(μ/E)∂V/∂q.
Thus the proper clocks are histories, and their energy exchange feeds back on
the inertial mass and trajectory. The allowed branch requires μ>0 throughout.
Variation of z gives the interval equation with endpoint force

    M_n z''+K_n z = −Σ_i g gap_i(t_i(σ)) e_i,
    χ_i(s_i(t_i(σ)))=σ.

Only the covered portion of each mouth history supplies this source. No clock
history is extrapolated. Outside that finite observation window the endpoint
has its stated free boundary. The endpoint source is not multiplied by ε:
the delta-function change of variables uses |dχ/dt|=s_dot. Temporal orientation
does enter k_dot. This distinction is essential to the energy check below.

## Reversals are separate operations

- **Closed surface:** conformal free propagation gives antipodal refocusing
  and a sign-reversed 2π return. No kick is scheduled at a focus.
- **Throat:** finite-interval dynamics and endpoint impedance produce repeated
  reflections. L determines travel time. No field is relaunched by hand.
- **Spatial parity:** η_B=−1 versus +1 is a twisted scalar-sector control.
  It is not a complete Klein/torus azimuthal gluing calculation.
- **Ordinary clock identification:** ε_A=ε_B=+1, b_A=b_B=0. Both mouths begin
  with s=0. An exact event transfer is χ_B(s_B(t_out))=χ_A(s_A(t_in))+path_length.
  A simultaneous clock difference is not substituted for t_out−t_in.
- **Time-reversed identification:** ε_B=−1, b_B=S (the neck history horizon).
  This is a genuine reversal of the event map, not a field sign flip. It
  introduces an explicit future anchor; it is NOT generated by ordinary
  differential aging from a synchronized birth. Its solution is a global
  history boundary-value problem. Change the anchor in a separate control.

The ordinary and reversed models are different physical assumptions. Neither
is selected by a fit to the desired trajectory. Unsupported emission/arrival
events are marked outside the computed history, never clipped into the past.

## Two complementary conservation checks

For a self-consistent neck history the autonomous exterior Hamiltonian H_ext
and meridional rotation charge J=Σp−PᵀDu are conserved. They include the clock
energies; simply adding H_neck to H_ext would double count a different time
generator and is not the claimed conserved quantity. On the full neck history,

    H_neck(S) − H_neck(0) + E_load = Σ_i ε_i [k_i(T)−k_i(0)].

This independently checks the source Jacobian, time-orientation sign and
clock/neck exchange. A passive endpoint load, used only as a reflection control,
has E_load=∫load Σ_endpoint z_dot² dσ. It is explicitly an external sink.
Neither check establishes a GR stress-energy tensor or a physical asymptotic
energy for a non-time-orientable spacetime.

## Frozen first experiment (before pilots)

Baseline: l_max=8, neck nodes=17, L=.565, mouth mass=1, flux=.5,
footprint=.20, scalar packet width=.35, packet energy=.02, g=.5,
q_A=.3, q_B=−.3, nominal birth velocities +.30 and −.30, k_A=k_B=s_A=s_B=0.
Nominal velocities specify p_i=m v_i/√(1−v_i²); the actual initial velocities
include the port contribution to μ and are reported. The scalar is a single
symmetric packet at birth, initially at rest. The neck starts at rest with
zero field at σ=0. Exterior horizon T=8; neck horizon S≥T. Sample spacing .02.

Cases: ordinary baseline; unequal birth momenta (.45,−.15); g=0; blocked
neck (z=0, conservative local ports); η_B=+1; reversed time map; reversed
map with S=9 instead of 8; passive endpoint load=1. Also tighter ODE/fixed-point
tolerances and higher resolution l_max=12, neck nodes=25, spacing=.01.
Comparisons keep the specified initial fields and momenta, not equal total
energy across different port Hamiltonians. Initial energies are reported.

Solve the two histories reciprocally, iterating neck endpoint displacement and
velocity until their sup differences are below 2e−7 (refined: 2e−8), with a
maximum of 80 relaxed iterations. Save every iteration residual. After the
fixed-point solve, require exterior energy relative drift <1e−5, angular drift
<1e−6 and full-history clock/neck mismatch <1e−5 of the initial scalar energy.
Report non-convergence, nonpositive μ, clock-domain failure and mouth contact.
Do not interpret a trajectory after contact as two separate regular mouths.
No topology surgery is introduced in this test.

Questions, with no assumed positive answer: does throat coupling change mouth
motion relative to g=0 and blocked-neck controls? Do spatial parity, internal
reflection loading and time orientation change it? Do supported ray transfer
events overlap the actual measured force history? Are results stable under
refinement? A changed trajectory is not by itself QED-like exchange, sustained
self-acceleration, a collapse mechanism or evidence of quantization.

Primary context: Morris, Thorne & Yurtsever (1988),
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.61.1446; Friedman & Morris (1991),
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.66.401. These motivate clock matching and
global wave boundary data; they do not derive or validate the constitutive
action above. The fixed-point solver must demonstrate its own residuals.
