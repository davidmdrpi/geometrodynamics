Compare to the independent radial integral dμ/dr = −r³(p²+f²)/3 at the symmetry center. Use at least two grids; last valid cells at different radii cannot establish divergence.
2. Use three distinct positive squared roots a = b(1−ε), b = 0.4, c = 2−a−b. The reference electrovac has Q²=abc/2 and μ=(ab+ac+bc)/2. Vary ε = 0.5, 0.2, 0.05, 0.01. Report κ at all three horizons and actual initial center mass. For each run sample (r²−a)/(b−a) at 0.5, 0, −0.5 and −1 where accessible.
3. Use nested grids for the packet. Record stop reason, both null constraints (maximum at center), integral mass, invariant mass, proper time, center speed, all inward-to-outward crossings and the subsequent outgoing interval. A resolved bounce requires consistent r_min and subsequent U>0 on two grids, bounded converging mass through the turn, and improving constraints. A finite mass increase at matching radii is evidence for physical amplification only. An exponential growth claim needs an independently normalized advanced clock and a converged growth interval. No global regularity or censorship verdict follows from a finite run.
