Pink/teal: inward/outward deformation. Gold: the finite contact region and illustrative rim-motion guides. A fixed radial half-thickness ε = 0.025 defines the contact band. The wave is prescribed, not solved; contact is detected from its geometry. Reconnection and motion after contact are regularized illustrations, not fluid dynamics.

What changed, and what is being assumed?

The undeformed profile is a fixed narrow cosh bridge, R₀(x) = b cosh[acosh(1/b) x/h], with h = 0.6. It does not shrink on a clock. A prescribed traveling packet adds a signed radial displacement. Contact is the first time min R(x,t) = ε, when the opposing display bands meet. No connected zero-radius surface is rendered.

The contact region is displayed over a finite width and blended into two rounded closed caps over 0.5 display-time units. Their shoulders then retract, with curved motion guides. This is an explicit visual regularization, not a proof that a smooth embedded surface can change topology while remaining everywhere regular. Surface thickness, coalescence and fluid flow would need a constitutive model to make that physical claim. No reconnection happens for the wave-off and weak-wave examples within the displayed horizon.

The cutaway removes a front sector for visibility. The contact close-up displays an axial section; its zoom follows the separating caps. Gold guides are not computed fluid velocities; no energy release or detached toroidal object is assigned. The resting narrow neck itself is assumed, not asserted to be a stable equilibrium catenoid.
