# Sub-gap loading of the electrovac bridge

Use the time-symmetric electrovac chain underlying #371 and the cold family of #372. Set the squared black-hole radius b=0.4 and inner radius squared a=b(1−g), primarily g=0.01. Start at the outer bifurcation sphere with zero normal areal velocity; follow its initially normal timelike geodesic. Benchmark its inward evolution against the exact electrovac orbit and its known turn in the analytic extension.

Set Δμ₀ equal to the reference electrovac mass minus the extremal cold mass at fixed Q. Prescribe an initial localized scalar energy shell outside the neck and define Δμ_scalar by the difference of the electrovac mass parameters on either side of the shell. Use ε=0, 0.005, 0.05 and 1.0 at fixed Q. Use the same radial energy profile for the standing and initially chiral data, and explicitly solve both constraints. Report integrated scalar momentum, the two initial null fluxes, and compatibility with the half-shift/time reversal. Include a matched chiral control at ε=0.05. Keep a globally one-way preparation on the time-oriented cover if the quotient reverses chirality. Report any wave-equation generation of the opposite component.

Compare nested grids, center proper time, signed areal velocity, geometric mass, constraint errors, null travel and the validity of the domain of dependence. Accept a bounce only for a negative-to-positive velocity crossing with bounded converging mass and a resolved outgoing interval on two grids. Disclose whether the same Cauchy solver resolves the vacuum turn. If a bounce is established, bracket its loss under independent variation of load and repeat at other g. Otherwise report what the Cauchy problem determines and the actual clock/radius coverage. A last grid cell, an analytic electrovac extension, or a one-sided null flux specified only on the initial slice cannot establish a matter threshold or an instability theorem.
